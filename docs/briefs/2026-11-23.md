## Macro Cycle Assessment

The macro regime remains **resilient growth, elevated inflation, and tightening financial conditions**, but market stress is now broad enough to warrant a more defensive tactical stance. Q3 real GDP grew at a 3.4% annualized rate and October payrolls rose 165,000 with unemployment at 4.1%, yet October industrial production fell 0.4% and capacity utilization declined to 76.4%. The growth signal is therefore positive but less uniform than the headline GDP figure implies.

Inflation and policy risks remain asymmetric. October import prices rose 1.1% after a 1.1% September increase. The October FOMC minutes said inflation pressures were broad based; almost all participants judged that further rate increases would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlook, and two members dissented in favor of an immediate 25-basis-point increase. The broad dollar index rose to 123.60 on November 20, tightening global liquidity even as the 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.92% in a defensive rally.

Risk assets have not confirmed a soft landing. The S&P 500 is down 2.4% over seven days and 5.1% over 30 days, Nasdaq-100 is down 1.7% and 7.6%, and Bitcoin is down 5.7% and 23.5%. VIX closed near 27 after reaching 35.89 in the prior session. Investment-grade and high-yield spreads widened to 98 and 376 basis points, respectively. Estimated flows favor cash, Treasuries, and investment-grade bonds while remaining negative across equities, gold, high yield, and crypto.

## Key Risks and Catalysts

- **GDP and PCE on November 25:** the Q3 second estimate, corporate profits, October spending, and inflation data can materially change the growth/inflation balance.
- **Beige Book on November 25:** regional evidence will test whether resilient national data are masking broader slowing.
- **Hawkish policy risk:** the minutes show a strong bias toward additional tightening, including discussion of a larger move if inflation disappoints.
- **AI and leverage:** the Fed highlighted elevated valuations, concentrated AI exposures, opaque financing, hedge-fund leverage, and the risk that weaker AI returns could amplify forced selling.
- **Credit confirmation:** further spread widening would validate the equity-flow warning; stabilization would support quality-credit carry and a tactical risk rebound.
- **Holiday liquidity:** Thanksgiving-shortened trading can amplify price moves and reduce the reliability of short-window signals.

## Limitations

ETF flows are model-derived estimates using price/volume direction and, when available, share-count changes; they are not official issuer-reported flows. Yahoo's shares-outstanding endpoint returned HTTP 401, so same-day share-change flow is unavailable and the series uses the OHLCV-derived proxy. Flow windows include populated sessions through November 23, while credit and Treasury data end November 20 and WTI ends November 17. Commodity coverage is incomplete and no clean commodity-flow proxy exists. Aggregate earnings revisions, valuation multiples, detailed market breadth, futures positioning, issuer default data, and regional macro detail are not integrated. Thanksgiving-shortened trading may reduce the reliability of near-term price and flow signals.
