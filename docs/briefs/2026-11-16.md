## Macro Cycle Assessment

The macro mix shifted toward stagflation risk rather than recession. October CPI rose 0.4% month over month and core CPI rose 0.3%. Because October 2025 indexes were unavailable after the prior lapse in appropriations, BLS could not publish many October year-over-year CPI comparisons. Producer inflation was less ambiguous: final-demand PPI rose 0.3% in October and 6.5% year over year, the highest annual rate since November 2022. Strong Q3 GDP and October payroll growth still argue against contraction, but the inflation data leave little room for near-term policy relief.

Financial conditions tightened. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.15%, the broad trade-weighted dollar increased to 122.65 on November 13, and VIX was about 20 intraday on November 16. The S&P 500 fell 1.8% over seven days, Nasdaq-100 fell 2.5%, and Bitcoin fell 12.3%. Credit spreads improved modestly over the week, but ETF positioning turned negative across equities, duration, high yield, and crypto. Cash remained the clearest persistent inflow.

The tactical regime is **resilient growth, renewed inflation pressure, and restrictive liquidity**. That favors cash and quality credit, argues for reduced long-duration growth and emerging-market risk, and limits conviction in Treasuries despite their defensive role.
