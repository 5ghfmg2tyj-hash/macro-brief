## Macro Cycle Assessment

The U.S. economy remains resilient but inflation-constrained. Real GDP expanded at a 3.4% annualized rate in Q3, with private domestic final sales up 4.9%, while October payrolls rose 165,000 and unemployment held near 4.1%. September job openings were stable at 7.5 million. Those readings argue against an imminent recession and support economically sensitive assets, but they also reduce the urgency for monetary easing.

Inflation is the binding risk. September headline CPI was 3.7% year over year and PCE inflation was 3.6%, while core PCE was 3.1%. On October 28 the Federal Reserve held the funds target at 3.75%-4.00%; two voters preferred another 25-basis-point increase. The broad trade-weighted dollar was 121.88 on November 6, little changed from a month earlier, so currency conditions are not providing a meaningful relief valve. The regime is therefore best described as **solid nominal growth with restrictive real rates, sticky inflation, and elevated event volatility**.

Cross-asset signals show defensive rotation beneath resilient growth. The S&P 500 was flat over seven days but down 3.3% over 30 days; Nasdaq-100 monthly momentum was materially weaker at -9.6%. Small caps and developed international equities outperformed, gold rebounded, Treasury yields declined, and cash continued to attract capital. VIX closed at 19.92 on November 9 after trading much higher during the prior session, confirming that drawdown risk remains above calm-regime norms.

## Key Risks and Catalysts

- **October CPI on November 10:** the largest immediate catalyst. An upside surprise would pressure duration and long-duration growth; a downside surprise would support both.
- **Treasury supply:** $42B of 10-year notes on November 10 and $25B of 30-year bonds on November 12 test duration demand.
- **Inflation-policy conflict:** strong growth and sticky inflation could keep the Fed restrictive even as risk assets weaken.
- **Breadth and concentration:** small-cap outperformance is constructive, but Nasdaq's weak monthly trend and outflows show that leadership remains unstable.
- **Credit and volatility:** HY spreads are 31 bps wider over one month, while VIX near 20 signals meaningful drawdown risk despite its November 9 decline.
- **Geopolitical and energy risk:** WTI remains well above its year-ago level, but the oil observation is stale and no clean commodity-flow proxy is available.
